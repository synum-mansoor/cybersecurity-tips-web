Tips for Financial Cybersecurity

Here are some tips to help you stay safe online:

Straight to the source

Phishing emails and texts can look like they came from your bank or a regulator. Skip the link. Open your bank's app or type its address into the browser yourself, and check there whether the message is real. Attachments from people you don't know get the same treatment.

Your bank won't ask for your password

Scammers also phone people and pose as bank staff. Woodlands Bank recently warned customers about this and said it never asks for online banking logins, Social Security numbers, or account details by call or text. If someone does, hang up and call the number on your card.

Give every account its own password

At least 10 characters and avoid names, birthdates, and common words. Reuse is the bigger risk, because one stolen password opens every account that shares it. A password manager keeps track of them for you.

Turn on multifactor authentication

MFA asks for a second proof, like a texted code or a fingerprint. A criminal with your password is still stuck. If your phone service suddenly cuts out and you get codes by SMS, treat it as a warning sign of SIM swapping.

Keep your software updated

Set your operating system, browser, and security software to update automatically. Old software is how malware gets in. Ignore pop-ups that claim they scanned your computer and found a virus, since scammers use them to sell fake protection.

Check for https and log out

Only enter financial details on pages that start with https, and look on every page, not just the sign-in screen. When you finish, click log out. Closing the tab can leave the session open.

Use your own device and a trusted network

Public computers can carry software that records keystrokes. Public Wi-Fi can be intercepted, and some hackers set up fake networks with familiar names. Wait until you're on a network you trust.

Turn on alerts and read your statements

Alerts for account changes and large purchases help you catch problems fast. Read statements as soon as they arrive. Watch for unfamiliar transactions, changed contact details, and missing statements, and call your bank right away if something looks off.

Back up your files

No system is completely secure. Copy important files to an external drive and keep it somewhere safe.

If you get hit

Change the password on the breached account and on any other account that used it. Consider a fraud alert or credit freeze, and report identity theft at IdentityTheft.gov if you're in the US.
